\begin{abstract}
Deleting a raw document can change which surviving documents enter training.
A model trained after semantic deduplication may therefore need examples that
were excluded from its original training set. We study this effect for a fixed
ordered suppression rule: a document is retained only when no earlier raw
document blocks it. We give a finite-horizon summary of additive training
statistics whose exact update equals reconstruction after deletion, with the
remaining deletion budget reduced accordingly. We also construct stored FP32
features for which increasing that budget by one changes the minimum private
information needed for exact ridge recovery from zero to $mD$ bits. The graph
is stable under a specified FP64 cosine scorer, and a sequential codec attains
the conditional private-information minimum on this family. These are scoped
algebraic and information results. An AskUbuntu calibration study uses two
local LLMs and no human annotation. Both completed calibration versions failed
their frozen selection criteria. In the follow-up, the largest weighted
joint-positive proportion over 51 cuts was 40\%, below the required 95\%.
The downstream effect study therefore remains unexecuted. No natural-text
utility, runtime, or total-memory advantage is claimed at this checkpoint.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Data curation determines what a learner sees. Semantic deduplication removes
examples judged too similar to earlier examples, often before training begins
\cite{semdedup}. A later deletion request acts on the raw data collection.
Repeating the same curator after that deletion can produce a different
training set, including documents that were never used by the original model.
An unlearning procedure must specify whether it promises to reproduce that
new training set and learner.

Let $D$ be a raw corpus, $F$ the records to forget, $\cur$ a curator, and
$A$ a learner. The target studied here is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target}
                    A\bigl(\cur(D\minus F)\bigr).
\end{equation}
Deleting $F$ from the already selected set instead gives
$A(\cur(D)\minus F)$. These targets can differ even when $F$ contains no
training example. For example, order three records $a\prec b\prec c$ and
place similarity edges only between $a,b$ and $b,c$. If every earlier raw
neighbor blocks a record, initially only $a$ is selected. Deleting the
excluded record $b$ admits $c$ (Figure~\ref{fig:chain}). The example is an
algebraic illustration; it is not a fabricated natural-language observation.

\end{minipage}}

This behavior raises two connected questions. What state supports exact
repair without rereading the surviving raw corpus? How much information can
be necessary about examples that remain excluded today but could enter after
future deletions? The allowed number of deletions matters to both questions.
With a limited cumulative budget, some examples can never lose all their
blockers, so their private payloads need not be retained for this target.

We make three scoped contributions. First, we specialize signed Boolean
provenance to the ordered curator and derive a summary that repairs both
training statistics and its own designated algebraic state. Second, we give
a finite-format construction linking excluded feature bits to exact ridge
heads, including a sharp change in required private information when the
deletion horizon increases by one. Third, we report two completed, negative
calibrations for a fully LLM natural-text study. Neither admitted a threshold
under its frozen model-agreement criterion. The downstream comparison of
target changes, utility, and complete system cost remains unexecuted.

Neither deletion-induced additions nor provenance maintenance is a new
general principle. Nonmonotone database queries already exhibit the former,
and provenance and incremental view maintenance address the latter
\cite{green,aggregate,dbsp,priu,ivmunlearning}. Our claim concerns the stated curator,
remaining-horizon repair contract, and shared-feature learner construction.
The main unresolved practical question is whether these effects are large
and useful enough in natural NLP pipelines to justify their full cost.

\section{Target, curator, and access contract}\label{sec:setup}
Each record $v$ has a stable identifier, a fixed feature row $z_v$, and, when
needed, a task label $y_v$. A fixed graph defines eligible similarity edges,
and a fixed total order $\prec$ defines priority. An edge may require a
fixed partition membership as well as cosine similarity strictly above a
threshold $\tau$. Define the earlier blockers
\[
                 B(v)=\{u:u\prec v,\ \{u,v\}\text{ is an edge}\}.
\]
The selection after cumulative deletion $F$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:activation}
 S_F=\cur(D\minus F)=\{v\notin F:B(v)\subseteq F\}.
\end{equation}
All earlier raw neighbors matter, including neighbors that the curator
rejects. This is not greedy selection against only previously retained
records. For nonnegative thresholds, the upper-triangle column-maximum
test in the released SemDeDup code has this suppression form
\cite{semcode}. We analyze its fixed graph and order; we do not identify
our target with every stage or configuration of that pipeline.

\paragraph{Restriction consistency.}
Under deletion, feature rows, pair eligibility, relative priority, and the
threshold remain unchanged on surviving records. A hash of each stable ID,
with an explicit ID tie break, can preserve the order. Rerunning a
corpus-dependent shuffle with the same random seed need not preserve it.
Refitting clusters or embeddings can change surviving edges and defines a
different counterfactual target. Such refitting requires separate oracles
and evidence; it is outside the exact theorem below.

\paragraph{Budget and eligible payloads.}
Fix an original cumulative record budget $K$. After forgetting $F$, the
remaining budget is $h=K-|F|$. A surviving record can appear under some
allowed continuation exactly when it belongs to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eligible}
       E(F,h)=\{v\notin F:|B(v)\minus F|\le h\}.
\end{equation}
Deleting all remaining blockers proves sufficiency; fewer deletions cannot
remove them all, proving necessity. Along a valid continuation, a record
outside this set cannot become eligible: its blocker count decreases by
at most the number of newly deleted records while $h$ decreases by exactly
that number. Existing selected survivors remain selected under this rule.

\paragraph{Three separate guarantees.}
Corpus recovery returns $S_F$. Model recovery returns the prescribed
learner on $S_F$. State recovery reconstructs a specified retained-data
state that supports further requests with budget $h$. The last guarantee
does not erase earlier outputs, backups, allocator history, or every
machine file. Our lower bound charges all usable private-data-dependent
state, including caches, tickets, remote stores, retained random seeds,
and earlier outputs available to the repair procedure. There is no free
reread of an excluded candidate. Public metadata is distinguished from
private information but remains part of physical cost.

\section{Exact repair of training statistics}\label{sec:method}
Let $t(v)\in\R^s$ be a fixed record-local statistic. It must be independent
of a model or feature extractor fitted to the deletable corpus when exact
state recovery is claimed. Introduce deletion indicators $x_u$ and write
$x_J=\prod_{u\in J}x_u$, including $x_\varnothing=1$. Then
\begin{align}
 T(x)&=\sum_v t(v)(1-x_v)x_{B(v)}=\sum_J\alpha_J x_J,\label{eq:poly}\\
 \alpha_J&=\sum_{v:B(v)=J}t(v)
       -\sum_{v:B(v)\cup\{v\}=J}t(v).\label{eq:coeff}
\end{align}
Each record contributes to at most two keys. This is a signed Boolean
provenance representation, building on established provenance ideas
\cite{green,aggregate}, rather than a new polynomial formalism.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-horizon state repair]\label{thm:repair}
Keep only coefficients with $|J|\le K$. For every $|F|\le K$,
\[
             \sum_{v\in S_F}t(v)=\sum_{J\subseteq F}\alpha_J.
\]
After an allowed request $F$, form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:update}
 \beta_L=\sum_{J:J\minus F=L}\alpha_J,\qquad |L|\le K-|F|.
\end{equation}
In exact arithmetic this is the same coefficient map as fresh construction
on $D\minus F$ with horizon $K-|F|$. Repeated requests therefore compose
independently of their batching and order, for the same final deleted set.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A monomial evaluates to one precisely when its key is contained in $F$;
larger-than-budget keys vanish. Substituting $x_u=1$ for $u\in F$ removes
deleted records through their $(1-x_u)$ factor and replaces each surviving
record's blockers by $B(v)\minus F$. A discarded degree-$K+1$ or higher
term loses at most $|F|$ variables, so it still exceeds the new cutoff.
Thus early truncation loses no term needed in the retained-data map.
Substitution commutes, and this argument also applies at every intermediate
remaining horizon.
\end{proof}

The comparison uses the remaining budget, not a fresh budget of $K$ after
every request. Replenishing the budget can require payloads that were
correctly discarded under the original contract. Exact algebraic equality
also does not imply bitwise equality of floating-point additions.

\paragraph{Indexed implementation.}
The P-I method stores nonzero coefficient keys, inverse incidences from
identifiers to keys, and degree buckets. Deleting $u$ touches only incident
keys, removes $u$ from each key, combines collisions, removes zero values,
and drops keys above the new horizon. The frozen empirical P-I interface
rejects repeated, already deleted, invalid, and over-budget IDs before
mutation. The earlier mathematical reference instead treats retries as
no-ops; both apply the same substitution to valid fresh deletions.
The reference P-S method scans all keys. Both implement
Equation~\eqref{eq:update} under the same numerical contract.

For initial key count $M_0$, total key length $L_0$, statistic dimension
$s$, and $Q$ supplied request IDs, define
$\Phi_0=\sum_{J}|J|(|J|+1)/2$. The indexed algorithm has sequence-wide
update cost
\begin{equation}\label{eq:work}
              O(Q+\Phi_0+sM_0)
              \subseteq O(Q+KL_0+sM_0),
\end{equation}
under expected constant-time dictionary operations apart from tuple
processing. Each key shrink consumes its triangular-length potential;
merges and pruning delete entries and incidences. There are at most
$L_0$ incidence touches and $M_0$ merges. Appendix~\ref{app:work}
gives the accounting. Construction, model solves, bit complexity, and
canonical serialization are additional costs. A single request can touch
all keys; there is no uniform sublinear per-request promise.

\paragraph{Ridge target.}
For $q$ responses and raw learner rows $z_v\in\R^d$, use
$t(v)=(z_vz_v^\top,z_vy_v^\top,1)$. Write the selected statistics as
$(G,H,n)$. Average-loss ridge with fixed $\lambda>0$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ridge}
                    (G+\lambda n I)W=H.
\end{equation}
The learner has no intercept, penalizes all coordinates, and returns zero
when $n=0$. The regularizer scales with the current selected count.
Features normalized for cosine scoring do not replace the stored raw
learner rows. A dense solve costs $O(d^3+d^2q)$, and each dense statistic
contains $O(d^2+dq)$ values. Storing these values can cost more than compact
eligible rows; arithmetic convenience alone is no memory advantage.

\paragraph{Source withdrawal.}
For a partition of records into sources, let $g(v)$ be a record's source
and $U(v)=\{g(u):u\in B(v)\}$. Replace the record contribution by
$t(v)(1-x_{g(v)})x_{U(v)}$ and apply Boolean idempotence. If
$g(v)\in U(v)$, the contribution vanishes: the record cannot survive the
withdrawal of its own blocking source. Otherwise the same substitution
and degree argument applies, now with a source-count budget. Mathematical
singleton sources do not demonstrate genuine contributor withdrawal.

\section{A finite-format memory obstruction}\label{sec:memory}
The previous algorithm establishes sufficiency. We next show why an exact
service may need information about excluded records even when the current
head, and every head under a smaller budget, is zero. The construction is
mathematical; no claim is made that a text encoder realizes these rows.

Fix $b\ge1$, $m\ge2$, and powers of four $k,D$ with
$d=2+k+D\le2^{20}$. Public signatures
$u_i\in\{\pm1/\sqrt{k}\}^k$ satisfy $|u_i^\top u_j|\le1/6$ for
$i\ne j$; orthogonal Hadamard rows suffice when $m\le k$.
Sort fixed public IDs by their actual priority and assign roles: an anchor
$a$, $b$ common blockers $S$, $m$ individual blockers $p_i$, and $m$
candidates $w_i$, in that order. Use orthogonal coordinate blocks
$e_0,e_1,U,H$ of dimensions $1,1,k,D$, and set $\alpha=1/128$.
Only the signs of $z_i\in\{\pm1/\sqrt D\}^D$ are private. The raw rows are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rows}
\begin{aligned}
 a&=e_0,\\
 s_j&=\tfrac12 e_0+\tfrac78 e_1,\\
 p_i&=\tfrac12e_0+\tfrac78u_i,\\
 w_i&=\tfrac12e_1+\tfrac78u_i+\alpha z_i.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Candidate labels are one; every other label is zero. All nonzero entries
are exactly stored normal FP32 values in the stated dimension range.
Candidate labels, graph, signatures, roles, and blocker payloads are public.
The entire public contract is fixed independently of the private signs.

At the stored binary64 threshold $\tau=\operatorname{float64}(0.4)$,
the frozen ordered FP64 normalization and dot product give
\[
                         B(w_i)=S\cup\{p_i\}
\]
uniformly over all private signs. Ideal required edges exceed $.43$;
required nonedges are below $.372$. The scorer error is below $2^{-32}$
under the explicit arithmetic assumptions in Appendix~\ref{app:scorer}.
This is a proof for that operation order, not a certificate for arbitrary
BLAS kernels, GPUs, or reassociated arithmetic.

Initially only the zero-label anchor is selected. At budget $b$, no
candidate can lose its $b+1$ blockers, and every target head is zero.
At budget $K=b+1$, the request $F_i=S\cup\{p_i\}$ selects $a,w_i$.
For exact rational ridge with rational $\lambda>0$, define
\[
                t=16641/16384+2\lambda.
\]
Since $a\perp w_i$, the resulting head is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hardhead}
 \theta_i=\frac{w_i}{t}
     =\frac{\tfrac12e_1+\tfrac78u_i}{t}+\frac\alpha t z_i.
\end{equation}
Its $H$ coordinates reveal all $D$ private signs of candidate $i$.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp private-information transition]\label{thm:bits}
On this fixed public family, the minimum deterministic exact model-query
state is zero private bits at horizon $b$ and $mD$ private bits at horizon
$b+1$. The initial state of an exact sequential service has the same lower
bound. It also holds for randomized services exactly correct almost surely
for every fixed input and query.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
There are $2^{mD}$ independent sign assignments. Any two differ on some
candidate $i$ and produce different heads on its public request $F_i$.
Their states must therefore differ. Conversely, storing all signs allows
reconstruction of the indicated head on $F_i$ and zero on all other
allowed queries. At horizon $b$, always returning zero suffices. There
are finitely many inputs and requests, so a randomized zero-error service
has a common probability-one set of coins on which the deterministic
distinguishability argument applies.
\end{proof}

Here a $\bits$-bit state has at most $2^{\bits}$ distinguishable values
conditional on public information and independent public coins. Usable
length or framing choices must also be charged. The probes delete only
public blockers, so access to their forgotten payloads does not bypass
the bound. This result concerns private information; public metadata,
padding, workspace, and output representation still consume bytes.
Every usable sign-dependent model, cache, ticket, retained random seed,
earlier output, or external store counts toward the private state. Repair
cannot reread retained candidate features or use an uncharged private
side channel. This access restriction is part of the theorem, not a
property established by hashing an input file.

For cumulative deletion $F$ under the original $K=b+1$, define
\begin{align}
 I(F)&=\{i:w_i\notin F,\ |B(w_i)\minus F|\le K-|F|\}\nonumber\\
     &=\{i:F\subseteq S\cup\{p_i\}\},\qquad e(F)=|I(F)|.\label{eq:live}
\end{align}
The equality uses the specific construction, not arbitrary graphs.

\begin{corollary}[Conditional retained state]\label{cor:live}
For a fixed admissible $F$, correctness for every private assignment and
every allowed continuation requires and is achievable with exactly
$e(F)D$ private bits.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Each $i\in I(F)$ can still be isolated by deleting its remaining blockers,
so its signs must be distinguishable. Storing just those words suffices.
Under further deletion $F'\supseteq F$, $I(F')\subseteq I(F)$, so a
discarded word never becomes relevant again within this horizon.
\end{proof}

The implemented finite-family codec packs these words in canonical order
and copies only surviving eligible words at each transition. Its immutable
export has a 48-byte header, the charged public deletion ledger, and
$\lceil e(F)D/8\rceil$ payload bytes. Fresh retained-data initialization
and sequential export produce the same bytes under the same public
universe and remaining budget. This is not a general compressor for
natural embeddings, physical erasure, or a claim about transcripts.
The conditional theorem fixes $F$; an adaptive request policy can itself
reveal private information through its requests.

\paragraph{Approximate heads.}
With expected squared parameter error at most $\varepsilon$ on each fixed
input and hard probe, a standard information argument gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rate}
 \bits\ge mD R_{\rm sq}\!\left(\frac{t^2\varepsilon}{4\alpha^2}\right),
\end{equation}
where $h_2$ is binary entropy in bits,
$R_{\rm sq}(v)=1-h_2((1-\sqrt{1-4v})/2)$ for $0\le v\le1/4$
and zero above that range. The public component of
Equation~\eqref{eq:hardhead} attains error $\alpha^2/t^2$ with no private
information. Appendix~\ref{app:information} derives the bound. These are
classical information tools specialized to this family, not a new general
coding theorem. Parameter fidelity does not establish constant NLP test
loss: averaging prediction error over hidden basis directions introduces
a factor of $1/D$.

\section{Fully LLM natural-text study}\label{sec:study}
The empirical objective is to measure whether the target difference in
Equation~\eqref{eq:target} matters in a real text pipeline and whether a
repair method has useful total cost. The active branch uses existing
AskUbuntu records, native tag labels, and two local model raters. It has
zero human annotation and zero human review. Earlier human-gated protocol
requirements remain unmet; this branch does not relabel model judgments
as human evidence or inherit acceptance of the historical primary study.

\subsection{Data and task configuration}
The prepared population contains 140,250 question records, split into
98,187 training, 20,996 calibration, and 21,067 test rows. Source grouping
uses native account metadata as a contributor proxy. Missing or community
owners remain explicit unknown singletons; these are not eligible targets
for genuine source-withdrawal requests. The prepared data contain 88,344
source units, including 1,522 unknown units. Source completeness means
surviving records after the fixed export and filtering rules, not every
historical, deleted, or private contribution.

The corrected April 2024 release is supported by a documentary provenance
chain and checksummed acquisition. This is not publisher cryptographic
attestation. The active development acceptance is separate from earlier
source gates. Raw text and account-level records remain private; the
public return reports contain bindings and aggregate counts.

The assembled E5 cache contains 140,250 rows of 768 FP32 coordinates,
produced and replayed in 32 CPU shards. E5 provides fixed pretrained text
representations \cite{e5}. The native prediction task uses 20 tags and
retains questions with all-zero target vectors. Five source-grouped
calibration folds selected $\lambda=10^{-5}$ from the six frozen values
$10^{-6},10^{-5},\ldots,10^{-1}$; exact ties select larger $\lambda$.
Out-of-fold calibration predictions fix one F1 threshold per tag, with
the stricter threshold on ties and a never-positive rule when no
calibration positive exists. Test labels and deletion outcomes are not
used for this configuration. Other feature representations need their
own task settings.

Before effect measurements, a deterministic one-tenth whole-source
reservation allocated 10,314 training and 2,138 test rows to development.
The remaining evaluation allocation contains 87,873 training and 18,929
test rows; calibration is unchanged. Every reserved source stays outside
later evaluation, even if a small pilot does not use all its rows.
The development targets of 1,000, 2,500, and 5,000 training rows include
the entire boundary source and are selected in a frozen order after the
guard. Current pre-guard counts are upper bounds. The original 100,000
and 200,000 panels are infeasible on this preparation; unavailable cells
must remain visible rather than be filled from held-out data.

\subsection{Model-defined threshold calibration}\label{sec:judges}
The raters are pinned Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Mistral-Nemo-Instruct-2407.
They receive complete original pair texts and the same fixed task, with
no IDs, cosine scores, tags, prior answers, or desired outcome. Each runs
offline on an admitted A100 with BF16, batch size one, greedy constrained
decoding, forced Flash attention, a 512-token output budget, and a
32,768-token total budget. Truncation, summarization, format repairs,
semantic retries, and fallback attention are disallowed. Source text is
untrusted input; the judges have no tools or retrieval.

A pair is positive only if both valid outputs label it either an exact
copy or substantially the same meaning. Other labels, disagreements,
uncertainty, and invalid or unavailable outputs contribute zero positive
mass while remaining in the full denominator. The models are not assumed
independent. Thus the target is a conservative two-model label function,
not semantic truth. Known LLM-judge biases motivate retaining order
diagnostics, but agreement cannot replace human validity
\cite{judge}.

Version 4 enumerates all 220,405,510 unordered calibration pairs,
including pairs within the same source. Thirteen right-closed cosine
strata use cuts $.50,.90,.91,\ldots,.99,.995$. Each nonempty stratum
contributes a simple random sample without replacement of up to 30
pairs. The realized selection has 360 canonical pairs and 36 swapped
diagnostics per model. Exact inverse-inclusion weights estimate the
joint-positive proportion above each of the 51 original thresholds
$.50,.51,\ldots,.99,.995$, always with the strict stored-float comparison
$\text{score}>\tau$. The least strict threshold with weighted proportion
at least $.95$ and nonzero support is selected.

Only a selected threshold proceeds to a fresh simple random sample of up
to 200 pairs from \emph{all} above-threshold calibration pairs. A one-sided
exact 95\% finite-population lower bound must reach $.90$. Any change in
a model's availability/status or valid category on canonical overlap
between selection and validation makes validation unavailable. Swapped
pairs are diagnostics, not extra votes. No retuning follows a failure.
The confidence bound concerns the fixed/potential model-label function
under the sampling model; observed replay stability does not establish
stability everywhere or accuracy against human judgments.

\subsection{Guard, requests, and baselines}\label{sec:design}
The full development guard is conditional on passed fresh model
validation. It compares every reserved training row against all original
calibration and test rows, removing training leakage using the locked
threshold and the original scorer. This uses features and IDs, not later
evaluation labels. The intended scope is
$10{,}314\times42{,}063=433{,}837{,}782$ pairs. The 18,929 evaluation-test
labels stay uninspected during development. Development utility may use
only the 2,138 reserved test rows.

The planned requests include uniform record deletion, uniform genuine
source withdrawal, deletion among initially excluded records, and a
label-blind graph search for high-amplification stress requests. Uniform
record controls match source requests by removed-record volume. Stress
search does not estimate ordinary user behavior and is not an optimal
adversarial audit. Request streams, feasible panels, and resource bounds
must be sealed before their effect outcomes; the historical registry is
not authorization to mark every planned family as executed.

O-G recomputes the fixed curator and learner from surviving input; O-T
uses correct selected IDs but rebuilds moments. P-I uses indexed
coefficient repair, P-S scans coefficients, and P-R uses rank-coordinate
summaries with its reconstruction work charged. The essential B-E
baseline retains compact eligible feature/label rows, blocker incidences,
and buckets, then makes batched signed moment updates. It is not forced
to store an outer product for every row. B-A retains all payloads.
B-F deletes from the initial selected set and is a target-mismatch
diagnostic, not a correctness oracle. The smallest natural pilot must
compare at least P-I, O-G, and optimized B-E under identical requests.

Primary task quality is native multilabel mean squared error, with
micro/macro F1 and mean average precision secondary. Report selected-set
admissions, parameter and prediction deviations from the fresh oracle,
and the quality difference of the frozen-selection target. A large
admission count alone does not establish useful model change.
Costs include feature/cache access, graph/index state, moments, gathered
rows, peak workspace, serialization, initialization, updates, and solves.
Incremental timing and complete lifecycle timing are separate. A speedup
over fresh graph reconstruction alone is insufficient evidence of an
advantage over a strong incremental baseline.

\section{Evidence available at this checkpoint}\label{sec:evidence}
\paragraph{Completed negative calibration.}
The earlier version 3 attempted all 198 model calls: 90 canonical pairs
and nine swapped diagnostics per model. It produced zero joint positives
among the 90 canonical pairs and no qualifying threshold. One canonical
Mistral response was invalid JSON and stayed in the denominator without
retry. All 51 weighted cuts were independently recomputed. Fresh
validation was therefore not run. This is a failure of the registered
model-defined selection criterion, not evidence that every population
pair is semantically distinct.

Version 4 is a separately versioned follow-up after that negative result;
it is not independent confirmation or a rescue of version 3. Both models
completed all 396 calls. Qwen produced 391 valid responses and five invalid
JSON responses; Mistral produced 396 valid responses. Separate exact-byte
audits verified prompt, token, and grammar identities without repeating
model calls. The prior Qwen audit's newline-normalization failure and all
original outputs remain preserved.

None of the 51 frozen cuts passed. The maximum weighted joint-positive
proportion was $2/5$, reached at $.99$ and $.995$, against the required
$19/20$. Independent arithmetic replay verified all 51 cuts and the
negative decision. Fresh validation was consequently not run. Even the
frozen favorable-imputation sensitivity calculation peaked at $8/15$:
it treats invalid or uncertain judgments as potentially positive but
preserves observed substantive negatives. This is not a confidence bound.
Table~\ref{tab:calibration} reports representative frozen cuts; the companion
ledger contains every cut and its exact rational arithmetic.

fixed representation, rater pair, rubric, and selection protocol; it does

\paragraph{Completed development preparation.}
Actual source/cache/task bindings and the source-disjoint development
allocation passed a separate implementation review. A resource pilot
scanned 262,144 real-cache cross-pairs and matched the original guard's

The scan kernel took 0.7783 seconds in that pilot. This does not establish

The inherited theory has separate algebraic, scorer, and codec reviews.

No completed natural-text repair/utility/cost experiment supports a

The original program's 21,332 prospective jobs are a design inventory,

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Curation and whole-pipeline targets.}
SemDeDup studies embedding-based redundancy removal \cite{semdedup}.
The released suppression computation motivates our fixed ordered rule
\cite{semcode}; a full curation pipeline may have additional data-dependent
steps. ARCANE combines exact-unlearning architecture with representative
data selection \cite{arcane}. Its Section~3.2 assigns no retraining cost
to a request outside the selected subset. Under our raw-neighbor rule,
such a request can instead admit surviving records and change the head,
as Figure~\ref{fig:chain} shows. This comparison concerns that specific
selection contract. GRACE studies gradient-guided
forget/retain coresets for behavioral LLM unlearning \cite{grace}; its
target differs from rerunning the original fixed curator in
Equation~\eqref{eq:target}.

\paragraph{Provenance and incremental computation.}
Polynomial provenance \cite{green}, provenance for aggregation and
difference \cite{aggregate}, DBSP's incremental query framework
\cite{dbsp}, and PrIU's provenance-based regression updates \cite{priu}
are direct antecedents. Schelter explicitly frames unlearning as view
maintenance and includes tokenization in the maintained dataflow
\cite{ivmunlearning}. His Section~3 links efficient repair to sparse
computational dependencies. Thus preprocessing-aware incremental unlearning
also precedes this work. Our Boolean expansion is a specialization.
The scoped claims add the cumulative-horizon state contract and the
learner-level finite-feature obstruction. We do not claim that negative
dependencies, moment updates, or deletion-induced additions originate here.

\paragraph{Information needed for unlearning.}
Learning-unlearning space complexity already studies memory under a
bounded number of deletions \cite{space}. In the full version,
Theorems~5.4 and~6.3 give budget-dependent upper and lower bounds for
realizability testing; Appendix~G connects this task to empirical risk
minimization. Horizon-dependent memory and reconstruction arguments
therefore precede our construction. Our theorem specifies the joint
curator, stored feature values, ridge outputs, and sequential state.
Ticketed schemes separately measure central auxiliary bits and
per-record tickets supplied with forgotten examples \cite{ticketed}.
Their Definitions~1--2 and Remark~3 require exact predictor recovery
after one deletion request. Our contract additionally specifies the
state for subsequent requests at the remaining horizon and charges
all usable private information, including tickets. These different
targets and access rules must accompany any comparison of bounds.
Recent work on predictive versus counterfactual auditing state also
overlaps with the broad memory-separation principle \cite{counterfactual}.
Its public code was inspected in the earlier literature audit, but its
full-paper proof and approximate-success semantics remain unresolved in
our comparison. We claim the stated fixed-scorer, shared-feature ridge
construction, not a new general separation between prediction and
counterfactual information. Information-distortion arguments are classical
\cite{shannon}.

\section{Limitations and responsible use}\label{sec:limits}
The theory assumes restriction consistency, fixed features, and a fixed
cumulative budget. It does not cover arbitrary reclustering, encoder
refitting, overlapping ownership, or nonconvex language-model fine-tuning.
The finite-family lower bound measures private information and exact
rational heads. It neither proves total-byte optimality nor shows that
natural text embeddings contain the constructed private sign patterns.
A floating solver needs an actual error certificate before an exact-head
lower bound transfers to its releases (Appendix~\ref{app:certificate}).

The LLM-only study can establish properties of its model-defined labels.
Shared training data and correlated biases can make both judges wrong;
agreement is not human validation. Invalid responses remain part of the
criterion, and order sensitivity is reported separately. Existing native
tags are dataset labels, not newly obtained human annotations. No human
review is planned, and no human-ground-truth claim is available.

Native account identifiers are contributor proxies. Export filters and
unknown ownership limit the scope of source withdrawal. Source text may
contain personal information, so bodies and account-level manifests stay
private. Redistribution and attribution requirements must be resolved
before any raw-data release. Canonical state equality does not imply
physical erasure, privacy of prior answers, or compliance with every
possible deletion obligation.

Finally, the main empirical study is unfinished. The working paper
cannot yet claim natural semantic value or competitive efficiency. Its
completed negative calibration and earlier unfavorable memory comparison
are part of the evidence. The manuscript's eventual conclusions must
follow the audited results rather than the desired outcome.

\section{Conclusion}
Under ordered raw-neighbor suppression, deleting an excluded record can
change the learner's training target. A cumulative deletion horizon gives
both an exact repair rule and a precise boundary on which excluded
payloads can still matter. The finite-format family shows that this
boundary can change required private information sharply, even when all
lower-budget heads are identical. Both fully LLM calibration versions
failed their frozen selection criteria. Consequently, the downstream
natural-text study remains unexecuted, and the practical NLP value of
these effects remains unresolved.

\section{Update accounting and state boundaries}\label{app:work}
For singleton deletion of $u$, first obtain the inverse-incidence handles
of keys containing $u$. For each entry, remove its incidence at $u$, its
old dictionary key, and its degree-bucket membership. Remove $u$ from
the key. If the reduced key is absent, move the same entry handle to it;
all surviving inverse references remain attached to that handle and are
not rebuilt. If the key already exists, merge the values and remove all
remaining incidences of the retired source entry. A zero result also
retires the destination and its incidences. Moving a value transfers its
reference; only merges perform vector arithmetic and a zero test. After
reducing the horizon by one, remove the now-excessive degree bucket.
No key gains an element, and the number of live keys never increases.

Give each initial incidence a token. A shrink removes a token; a merge
or prune removes its source tokens. None are created. Thus at most $L_0$
identifier touches and incidence removals occur over the whole sequence.
Each merge decreases key count, giving at most $M_0$ vector additions.
A tuple shrink from size $j$ costs $O(j)$, and decreases its potential
$j(j+1)/2$ by $j$. This charges tuple copying, hashing, and comparisons
under the stated dictionary model. Input handling costs $O(Q)$, including
repeated IDs. The resulting bound is Equation~\eqref{eq:work}.

The coefficient values and incidence metadata occupy
$O(sM_0+M_0+L_0)$ words, plus membership storage. Rational arithmetic
requires additional bit-length accounting. A canonical export removes
zero keys and orders keys and values, with at least linear output cost.
The fast in-memory structure is canonical only as a logical map modulo
handles, not as allocator memory. A final exact map can be equal to a
fresh construction while both a floating-point accumulation order and
its serialized bytes differ. Numerical checks against a fresh oracle
must therefore state their contract rather than borrow exact equality.

\section{Finite-family graph and scorer proof}\label{app:scorer}
All statements in this appendix concern the mathematical rows of
Equation~\eqref{eq:rows}, not natural encoder outputs. Powers-of-four
$k,D$ make $1/\sqrt{k}$ and $1/\sqrt D$ dyadic. The dimension cap keeps
every nonzero stored value in the ordinary normal range; the range argument
below also excludes nonzero subnormal intermediates in scoring.
The norms, independent of private signs, are
\[
 \norm a^2=1,\quad \norm{s_j}^2=\norm{p_i}^2=65/64,
 \quad \norm{w_i}^2=16641/16384=(129/128)^2.
\]
The anchor blocks every $s_j$ and $p_i$ since its cosine with them is
$4/\sqrt{65}>.49$. It has zero dot product with every candidate.
Common-blocker/candidate cosine is
$448/(129\sqrt{65})>.43$; individual-blocker/own-candidate cosine is
$784/(129\sqrt{65})>.75$. For $i\ne j$, the individual-blocker/candidate
cosine is at most $784/(6\cdot129\sqrt{65})<.126$.
Candidate-candidate cosine is at most
\[
 \frac{1/4+49/(64\cdot6)+1/16384}{16641/16384}
                 =\frac{18563}{49923}<.372.
\]
These inequalities cover every earlier candidate blocker decision.
Additional edges among the public blockers do not affect the proof,
since the anchor initially suppresses them and they have zero labels.

The exact stored threshold is
\[
 \tau=\frac{3602879701896397}{2^{53}}
      =\frac25+\frac{1}{5\,2^{53}}.
\]
For this family, raw squared coordinates and partial norm-square sums
are exact in binary64: they are multiples of $2^{-34}$ and have
magnitude below two. Assume round-to-nearest ties-to-even, correctly
rounded square root and division, and the specified coordinate order,
without arithmetic-changing reassociation or clipping. With
$u=2^{-53}$, set
\[
 \rho=\frac{2u}{1-u},\qquad
 \gamma=\frac{(d+1)u}{1-(d+1)u}.
\]
The normalized coordinate relative error is at most $\rho$. Products
therefore contribute at most $2\rho+\rho^2$ in absolute cosine error,
using Cauchy--Schwarz. Ordered dot-product rounding contributes at most
$\gamma(1+\rho)^2$. Hence
\[
E_d=2\rho+\rho^2+\gamma(1+\rho)^2<2^{-32}
                    \qquad(d\le2^{20}).
\]
To justify the relative-rounding model even under cancellation, nonzero
computed normalized coordinates have magnitudes between $2^{-18}$ and
two. Rounded nonzero products are at least $2^{-37}$ and are multiples
of $2^{-89}$. Ordered sums remain on that grid: rounding either preserves
the value or moves to a coarser subgrid. A sum is therefore zero or has
magnitude at least $2^{-89}$, above the subnormal range. The crude bound
$4d\le2^{22}$ excludes overflow. These facts cover the norm, division,
product, and accumulation range assumptions used above.
This cannot cross the edge/nonedge margins. It proves the uniform graph
under the stated scorer, independently of the $mD$ private signs.
Native runtime admission and implementation replay remain separate checks.

Public signature existence is also qualified. Hadamard rows give the
stated orthogonal case. Alternatively, independent normalized signs have
pairwise tail probability at most $2e^{-k/72}$. A union bound gives a
code when $k\ge\lceil72\log(2m(m-1))\rceil$, choosing an admitted
power of four that still obeys the dimension cap. This does not permit
arbitrarily many signatures at fixed dimension.

\section{Squared-error information bound}\label{app:information}
Let $Y_{ij}$ be independent uniform bits and express the hidden signs as
$2Y_{ij}-1$. There are $n=mD$ bits. Given a state $M$ and independent
public coins $R$, rescale an approximate hard-probe head by
\[
 \widehat Y_{ij}=\frac12\left(1+
                       \frac{t\sqrt D}{\alpha}\widehat\theta_i[H_j]\right).
\]
Clipping to $[0,1]$ cannot increase squared error, and
\[
 \frac1D\sum_j(\widehat Y_{ij}-Y_{ij})^2
 \le\frac{t^2}{4\alpha^2}\norm{\widehat\theta_i-\theta_i}^2.
\]
Posterior means $p_l=\Pr(Y_l=1\mid M,R)$ minimize conditional squared
error, even if a decoder uses fresh randomness. Thus average posterior
variance is at most $v=t^2\varepsilon/(4\alpha^2)$. For
$0\le s\le1/4$, the function
\[
 g(s)=h_2\!\left(\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4s}}2\right)
\]
is increasing and concave. One derivative check substitutes $s=q(1-q)$;
the needed sign follows from
$r-1/r-2\log r\ge0$ for $r\ge1$, whose derivative is
$(r-1)^2/r^2\ge0$ and value at one is zero. Since
$h_2(p)=g(p(1-p))$, conditional subadditivity and Jensen give
\[
 H(Y\mid M,R)\le\sum_l\mathbb E h_2(p_l)\le n g(v).
\]
The state alphabet contract implies $H(M\mid R)\le\bits$, so
$\bits\ge I(Y;M\mid R)\ge n[1-g(v)]$, proving
Equation~\eqref{eq:rate}. For $v\ge1/4$ the zero bound applies. Correctness
averaged over uniform signs and a uniform hard-probe index already
suffices. Averaging over all valid deletion sets is weaker because many
produce a zero head and can dilute the error.

The zero-information threshold is attained by returning just the public
component of the hard-probe head. Its squared error is $\alpha^2/t^2$
for every sign assignment. The repository also gives a finite covering
code upper bound for a query summary. It can require an enormous public
codebook and does not give an efficient sequentially erasable lossy codec;
we make no such implementation claim here.

\section{Finite-output and utility limits}\label{app:certificate}
The hidden coordinate magnitude in a hard-probe exact head is
$a_H=\alpha/(t\sqrt D)$. A deterministic certified parameter radius
strictly below $a_H$ identifies every hidden sign and transfers the
exact $mD$ private-bit bound. If $0<\lambda\le1$, then $t<4$ and
$\sqrt D\le2^{10}$, giving $a_H>2^{-19}$. An actual radius at most
$2^{-20}$ is therefore sufficient. A configured tolerance is not a
certificate: the radius must cover moment accumulation, solving, and
release rounding relative to the prescribed exact target. We have not
established such a certificate for the ongoing natural-text learner.

For a public test distribution uniformly sampling an $H$-basis vector,
prediction discrepancy is $\norm{P_H e}^2/D$ for head error $e$.
Uniform sampling of all coordinate basis vectors instead yields
$\norm e^2/d$. Consequently a constant parameter-error obstruction does
not establish constant task risk. Native tag prediction, natural source
requests, and matched-cost baselines are needed to assess practical value.

Framework and exact theorem (PDF p.2, Section III-A; p.3, Section V)
The state is a deterministic function f from a finite history set to a state
alphabet. Its cost is log2|f(Histories)|, a log-cardinality measure, not a measured
physical byte count; an integer fixed-width code would require a ceiling.
Definition 1 defines exact sufficiency via a decoder q=g composed with f.
Definition 2 defines the sufficiency closure. The common-refinement statement is
LEMMA 3, not Definition 3. No randomized encoder or sequential state-update
contract is specified by these definitions.

Definition 7 takes uniform H in {0,1}^t and Y_k(H)=1[sum H>=k] for k=0,...,t.
Outcome counterfactuals flip one recorded event: q_i,k(H)=1[s+1-2e_i>=k], where
s=sum H. Theorem 8 concerns these outcome queries, not the pivotality family.
The full predictive answer vector determines s and vice versa, hence exactly t+1
classes. For the full counterfactual tuple, kstar_i=max{k:q_i,k=1}=s+1-2e_i.
For a nonconstant history, max/min recover s and then each bit. Constant histories
have kstar 1 and t-1, distinguishable precisely when t>=3. Thus the map from
histories to complete answer tuples is injective and exactly 2^t states suffice
and are necessary. The t=2 exception is explicitly acknowledged. This argument
is finite and deterministic; no continuity premise or task-risk metric is used.

Approximation quantifiers (PDF p.3, Theorem 8(iii), proof (iii), Remark 9)
The justified event is
  P_(H uniform)[ (g_i,k(f(H)))_(i,k) = (q_i,k(H))_(i,k) ] >= 1-delta,
for 0<=delta<1, with fixed deterministic f and decoders. Each state supplies one
complete tuple. Injectivity permits at most one fully correct history per state,
so M states can succeed on at most M/2^t of the histories. Therefore
  M >= (1-delta)*2^t, log2 M >= t+log2(1-delta).
Integer state counts require M>=ceil((1-delta)*2^t). The probability here is over
uniform histories. This is joint exact-tuple success, not expected squared
parameter distortion, per-input random approximation, or separate accuracy for
each query. The supplied framework does not establish a general randomized-state
or adaptive-query theorem; extensions should be derived separately, not attributed.

Why the distinction is material
The weaker reading is: for every fixed (i,k),
  P_H[g_i,k(f(H))=q_i,k(H)] >= 1-delta.
Different queries may fail on different histories. The proof's one-successful-
history-per-state argument does not follow from this weaker condition.
An analytic witness (no experiment) separates the readings: set t=4, retain only
s=sum H (five states), and answer each query by g_i,k(s)=1[s>=k]. Let r be the sum
of the other three bits. Its answer differs from the actual flipped answer
exactly when r=k-1, so each query's error probability is
  binom(3,k-1)/8 <= 3/8
(with out-of-range binomial coefficients zero). Every fixed query therefore has
success at least 5/8, but the weaker reading of the claimed formula would require
(5/8)*16=10 states, exceeding the actual five. This refutes only that separate-
per-query interpretation, not the joint-success theorem supported by the proof.
The paper's Section IX (p.5) likewise characterizes its definitions as exact and
leaves a general divergence-based epsilon-sufficiency calculus to future work.

Recommended bounded related-work wording
Tadakamalla et al. (2026a, Section III and Theorem 8) formalize query-relative
sufficiency and give a binary-history threshold family with t+1 predictive states
and 2^t counterfactual states for t>=3. Their proof of Theorem 8(iii) gives a
(1-delta)2^t state lower bound when the complete counterfactual answer tuple is
correct with probability at least 1-delta under uniform histories. We use that
joint-success interpretation, rather than separate accuracy for each query.
Our expected squared head-error guarantee uses a different output and loss
contract. Our contribution is the fixed-scorer, shared-feature ridge construction
and its canonical sequential state at a shrinking deletion horizon, not a new
general separation between predictive and counterfactual information.

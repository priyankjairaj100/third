1. Tadakamalla, Surisetty and Asad (2026)
Predictive State Is Not Explanatory State: An Exponential Memory Separation
Between Forecasting and Counterfactual Auditing. DOI: 10.2139/ssrn.7520178.

The public reference implementation was inspected at version 1.0.0, commit
4143f408843394e21a0622c9eb5d5de10a687a8c:
https://github.com/PranayTadakamalla/predictive-state-not-explanatory-state/tree/4143f408843394e21a0622c9eb5d5de10a687a8c
Queries.py and decoder.py define a binary history H of length t, sum s, predictive
queries 1[s>=k], and counterfactual queries 1[s+1-2H_i>=k], for all thresholds
k=0,...,t. Predictive answers distinguish t+1 counts. The complete counterfactual
answer tuple recovers t bits for t>=3. Its threshold-transition indices are
s+1-2H_i; the constant-history cases have indices 1 and t-1. This is an exact
code/algebra comparison, not verification of the missing full paper's proofs.

Third's hard queries delete public blockers and return ridge heads on the same
stored features used for curation; its continuing-state contract specifies
fresh-state equality at the remaining deletion budget. The inspected code
provides a concrete antecedent for task-dependent information requirements and
exact reconstruction. It does not establish the scope of the full paper's
state/access model. We claim no new general predictive-counterfactual separation.

The advertised approximate memory expression cannot be numerically compared to
Third's expected squared parameter-error bound without its success event and
quantifiers. A per-query guarantee and joint recovery are different contracts.
Code-generated plots are not a substitute for the missing theorem. Full names
are taken from author-supplied citation metadata; software has its own pinned
reference. No full-paper novelty, exclusion or superiority clearance is claimed.

Assessment
The manuscript is in TMLR's scope as a theoretical study of a learning pipeline.
Its statements distinguish exact theorems, private-information bounds, failed
model-only calibration, and development diagnostics. I found no fatal theorem
gap in this read. This is not a formal proof certification or acceptance forecast.
Neither absent utility gains nor modest significance alone requires rejection;
the technical claims must remain supported and understandable.

Concrete fixes recommended
1. Flatten the supplement. V7 currently wraps V6, V5, V4, V3 and V1, leaving
   useful code six archive levels deep. Historical READMEs describe superseded
   source and literature states. Make one current README, one current source,
   directly accessible code, and separate calibration/structure/repair evidence
   directories. Preserve scientific bytes and source bindings. Keep developmental
   source deltas and historical author-to-agent instructions out of the upload.
2. Publish exact public judge system/task prompts, judgment/output schemas, and
   a clearly labeled public protocol view. The present supplement provides no
   prompt text despite model-defined negative results being central. Full raw
   text, IDs, sample observations and model outputs should remain excluded.
3. Explain the replay boundary prominently: aggregate arithmetic is runnable;
   private inference, raw graph/state regeneration and full empirical study are
   not. A receipt checker must not be called scientific replication.
4. Add publisher-style license attribution for the embedded TMLR style to the
   source supplement (existing TMLR_STYLE_LICENSE.txt is available). Scientific
   code is inspectable; distribution rights still need author confirmation.
5. Citation audit should pin the SemDeDup code commit and use actual publication
   metadata rather than inspection year. Other agents are checking each source.
6. Condense internal workflow terms (Phase 5/6, job inventory, inherited reviews)
   in the main text where possible. They should not obscure the actual API,
   results, and limitations. This is a clarity improvement, not an invitation
   to remove failed outcomes or erase the boundary of unexecuted work.

Official policy findings (retrieved 22 October 2026)
https://jmlr.org/tmlr/editorial-policies.html
The editorial screen includes scope, clarity/quality, unsupported claims and
format. No reuse of text, figures or results from archival or simultaneous
submissions is allowed. Author quota and exact author-list rules apply. LLMs
may assist, but authors remain responsible and LLMs cannot be authors.
https://jmlr.org/tmlr/author-guide.html
Use the TMLR PDF style and anonymize both paper and supplement. ZIP/PDF
supplement is limited to 100 MB. Submission licensing is CC BY 4.0. The guide
allows any paper length; 12 pages is not a hard maximum.
https://jmlr.org/tmlr/faq.html
The FAQ requires first-page AI-use disclosure and expresses an expectation of
human-sourced ideas, claims and results. More than 12 main pages affects review
timescale. A public preprint does not by itself violate double-blind review.
https://jmlr.org/tmlr/acceptance-criteria.html
Support and clarity of claims and interest to some ML readers are the criteria;
state of the art, large impact and methodological novelty are not necessary.
https://jmlr.org/tmlr/ethics.html
Human-derived data require thoughtful handling of consent, personal information
and harm. A new human annotation exercise is not a prerequisite for these issues.
